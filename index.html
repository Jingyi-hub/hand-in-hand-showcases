Origin & research framing

The project starts from curiosity about rural revitalization challenges such as talent outflow and limited aesthetic-education resources, then frames a research question using the PEO approach (Population–Exposure–Outcome).

  • Population: rural communities (e.g., left-behind children), grassroots cadres, returnee youth pioneers
  • Exposure: “educational empowerment” interventions (aesthetic education outreach, new media communication)
  • Outcome: improved aesthetic literacy, measurable social impact via media analytics

Research question: To what extent can an educational empowerment model stimulate endogenous motivation and cultural vitality in rural communities?

Goals

Direct actionEducation & donations
Improve learning and cultural experiences for left-behind children through aesthetic education and material support.
Social influenceNew media
Create rural-themed content (e.g., “Youth Helping Agriculture”) to expand public attention and participation.
Long-term strategyReplicable model
Summarize a scalable “education empowerment” model to support broader talent revitalization & cultural activation.

Core model: Four-Phase Education Empowerment

The team operationalizes a mature framework — “study in / learn from / learn for / enable the countryside” — and injects Gen‑Z energy and new media storytelling into practical implementation.

Study inImmersive field study
Live and learn in rural contexts to build empathy and scientific understanding of local realities.
Learn fromLocal wisdom
Interview grassroots cadres, Party members, and returnee pioneers to learn community resilience and solutions.
Learn forService & responsibility
Teach arts & culture classes, and support tech‑driven poverty alleviation, turning learning into public value.
EnableActivate endogenous motivation
Use a new‑media matrix to tell rural stories and amplify community vitality and sustained engagement.

Reflection

By reviewing the project cycle, the team connects practice experience with research-oriented teaching processes: theory foundation, comparative thinking, field investigation, participatory iteration, differentiated solutions, and multi‑stakeholder evaluation.

What workedStrengths
Policy tailwinds, Gen‑Z perspective, multi‑region coordination, “hand in hand” structure.
What to improveWeaknesses
Early-stage field experience challenges addressed by inviting mentors and senior media professionals.
